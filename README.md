## What you are actually buying

AkileCloud sells high-bandwidth KVM VPS with 1 Gbps to 5 Gbps ports, run on Proxmox with a self-built control panel rather than WHMCS or SolusVM. Every node comes with DNS-based streaming unlock by default, and the panel is the same across locations, so a Taiwan box and a Los Angeles box are managed the same way.

The catalogue is wider than the four or five plans most coupon pages list. The company claims 50+ nodes across 18 countries, with its own lines in Hong Kong, Japan, Taiwan, Singapore, the US, Germany, the UK, Italy and the Netherlands, plus a slower "Light Server" line on 5–30 Mbps links covering Southeast Asia, the Middle East and South America. Treat the country count as a vendor claim; what matters to a buyer is that Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore and Los Angeles are the lines with the most plan variety.

Two limitations show up on every source: mainland China direct connections are not guaranteed, and traffic is counted in both directions (upload plus download).

## Where the code actually goes

Two redemption paths are documented and they are not the same screen.

The account-side path: **Finance Center → Coupons → Redeem a code**, paste, confirm. The coupon lands in your Coupon List with its discount, status and expiry date. When you place a new VPS order, a Coupon selector appears at checkout after you sign in, and that is where the discount gets attached to the order.

The product-side path some guides describe: a redemption code field on the product detail page, usually top right, filled before checkout.

Both exist in the documentation; the account-side one is described in more detail and is the one to try first. If the field is not where a guide says it is, the panel has probably changed, and the Coupon List is the authoritative place to check whether the code actually landed.

Two things the coupon centre will not do. Coupons there apply to VPS products only, and the centre explicitly excludes **Light Cloud, dedicated servers and AI Gateway**. Coupon status also matters: Not Used, Used, Expired, Reserved by order, Revoked. A coupon showing "Reserved by order" is tied to an order you started and abandoned, and it will not attach to a second one.

## The part that changes what you pay next year

AkileCloud issues coupons in two modes, and the difference only shows up at renewal.

**Ongoing renewal discount:** applied to an existing server through an "Apply to server" action, it permanently changes that machine's renewal price and is redeemed the moment you apply it. There is no undo, so check the receiving server in the selection dialog before confirming.

**One-time renewal discount:** reduces the single renewal you select it for and nothing after that.

New-order coupons at checkout behave like the first mode or the second depending on how the individual coupon was issued, which is exactly why the coupon rules matter more than the headline percentage. A 20% one-time discount is a one-off; the same 20% as an ongoing discount is a permanent price change.

Annual billing is a separate saving that exists before any coupon. JPHyper-Starter is ¥49.99/mo, or ¥499.99 for a year. Paying monthly for twelve months costs ¥599.88. On the cheaper plans the gap is smaller in absolute terms but similar in proportion: TWLite-Mini at ¥249.9/yr against ¥24.99/mo works out to about ten months for twelve.

## Refunds, renewals and the coupon trap

Refunds go to account balance, never back to the original payment method. Within 24 hours of provisioning and with no more than 5 GB of traffic used, a full refund to balance is available, capped at three machines per account per calendar month and one machine per node per month. Setup fees, special configuration fees and leftover prepaid balance are never returned. The English service agreement declines refunds once service begins, which is the stricter version of the same policy.

Discounted plans and anything bought with a coupon cannot be refunded at all. That exclusion sits alongside the 24-hour window rather than inside it, and it is the single most expensive thing to miss on this page. Special-priced machines have their own "no refunds accepted" acknowledgement you have to tick before paying, so the warning is on screen at checkout.

Renewal runs automatically three days before expiry and needs enough balance in the account. After the paid period ends, the agreement provides for automatic, irreversible deletion. Traffic Auto Renew is a separate switch: when traffic runs out, it deducts the reset fee (¥7.00 for a traffic reset) from your balance instead of letting the machine sit throttled.

Payment methods include account balance, Alipay, WeChat Pay, UnionPay, international cards and USDT on TRC20 or Polygon. Some channels add a handling fee, and exchange-rate differences and bank charges are on you. Paid orders can be exported as a PDF invoice.

## FAQ

**Can I stack two AkileCloud coupons on one order?**
Every coupon carries its own plan, billing cycle and stacking rules, and checkout applies one deduction to the service price. Assume no unless the coupon's own terms say otherwise.

**Can I use the monthly LAX Pro code on an annual order?**
No. AkileCloud/LAXPRO-MONTH/CUT-20 is scoped to monthly LAX Pro; annual LAX Pro has its own code at 30%.

**Does the discount stay after the first billing cycle?**
Only if the coupon was issued as an ongoing renewal discount. Ongoing coupons applied to a server change its renewal price permanently; one-time coupons reduce one renewal.

**Can I refund a VPS bought with a coupon?**
No. Coupon and promotional-price orders are excluded from refunds entirely, including the 24-hour window.

**What happens when I hit the traffic limit?**
Traffic counts upload plus download. Once the allowance is gone, the port is throttled to 10 Mbps; a traffic reset costs ¥7.00 and can be handled manually or through the Traffic Auto Renew switch.

**Is there a China-optimised plan?**
LAX Pro runs CN2 GIA, Unicom 9929 and Mobile CMIN2. AkileCloud still does not guarantee direct mainland connections on any plan, so treat it as better routing rather than a promise.
